Belowthe Floor

Put $100,000 into a segregated fund and the insurer promises you'll get at least $75,000 back in ten years, whatever the market does. If you die first, your family gets the full $100,000.

That promise costs nothing in good futures and a lot in bad ones. So the only way to price it is to imagine thousands of futures and count. Every line below is one of them, simulated in your browser by the same model, with the same random numbers, as the GPU engine behind this page.

Each trail is the fund in one future, month by month, after fees. The red line is the floor. Futures that finish below it are where the insurer pays the difference. Hover to read the spread at any year.

IV

A future inside a future

Capital rules ask a harder question: a year from now, in a bad market, what will the remaining promise be worth? Answering it means simulating a real-world year, then, from wherever that lands, a whole new set of futures to value what's left. One path in, hundreds out, for every scenario.

V

Four million futures, four ways

Behind this page is a Python project that runs the same model on a hand-fused GPU kernel, on PyTorch with and without its compiler, and on plain NumPy. The race replays their measured times for 4,194,304 futures of 120 monthly steps, in real time.

segfund‑mc

How this page works

The browser engine (web/engine.js) is a line-by-line port of the project's NumPy reference. It draws the same Philox4x32-10 random numbers, so path 17 at month 40 is the same future here as on the GPU. A test checks it against the Python engine: the random words match exactly, and per-path values agree to about 1e-11.

What the numbers are

Prices use 16,384 futures per run (antithetic pairs and a Hull-White put control variate). The race and nested figures are measured results from the repository, not computed here.

What it isn't

An educational model. It is not actuarially approved, and every assumption is illustrative: one-factor rates, textbook mortality, an invented lapse rule. Source, validation and the full write-up are on GitHub.
